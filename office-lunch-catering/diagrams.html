FIG. 4.1

Daily ordering — collaboration

Two pools separate the organisations; lanes separate responsibility. Timer events carry the scheduled automation, the exclusive gateway decides whether the vendor is notified of a change, and dashed arrows are message flows across the pool boundary.

Tinted boxes are automated steps. The exclusive gateway after aggregation is what keeps the vendor from being spammed: a delta notice goes out only when the locked count differs from the H-1 estimate.
FIG. 4.2

Monthly reconciliation — collaboration

The draft is computed from confirmed delivered quantities, so the only human work is the days that disagree. The investigation loop returns to the matching step, and every adjustment is written to the audit trail before approval.

Reconciliation is quantity-based, not chat-based: the system compares what the vendor claims with what Admin confirmed at handover, and only the difference needs a decision.
FIG. 7.1

Entity relationship diagram — 16 tables

Two link tables carry the many-to-many allergen matching that elimination depends on. Two columns exist to protect history: orders.harga_saat_pesan freezes the price at order time, and invoice_items stores ordered and delivered quantity side by side so a difference stays auditable per day, per menu.

Fourteen domain tables plus the two operational ones — audit_logs and notifications — that the audit-trail and notification requirements depend on.
FIG. 9.1

Use case diagram — six actors

Two of the six actors are systems. Grouping the diagram by actor shows the shape of the product: the employee interaction is deliberately small, the Admin owns operations and money, and the Scheduler carries the automation that used to be manual work.

Confirming the delivered quantity is the one use case Admin and Vendor share — it is also the number every rupiah of the monthly invoice is derived from.
